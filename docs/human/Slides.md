  Does it make sense on Solana specifically?
                                            
  Yes, and it's actually a good fit for three reasons:
                                                                                                                                                         
  1. Native BN254 syscalls — Solana has hardware-accelerated BN254 elliptic curve operations built into the runtime. Verifying a Groth16 proof costs     
  ~280K CU ≈ $0.0003. On Ethereum this costs ~200K gas ≈ $3-10. Solana is 10-1000x cheaper for ZK verification.                                          
  1. Fast finality — Solana confirms in 400ms. The solver doesn't wait minutes to know if their proof was accepted. On Ethereum, a ZK proof submission   
  might sit in mempool for minutes and cost $10 in gas.                                                                                                  
  1. USDC native — SPL token transfers are first-class. The atomic "verify + pay" is clean.
                                                                                                                                                         
  The one honest weakness: Solana's 1232-byte transaction limit is tight. For checkers with complex outputs (large public values), it breaks. Workaround 
  is writing proof to an account first then verifying from account — doable but annoying.                                                                
                                                                                                                                                         
  ---                                                                 
  Is this genuinely novel? Could it be a startup?
                                                                                                                                                         
  Genuinely novel: yes. The specific combination of:
  - ZK proof submission as the answer format                                                                                                             
  - Any Rust checker as the verifier                                                                                                                     
  - Atomic verify+pay on Solana                                                                                                                          
                                                                                                                                                         
  ...doesn't exist anywhere right now. SP1 on Solana is weeks old. The ecosystem is wide open.                                                           
                                                                                                                                                         
  But is it a startup?                                                                                                                                   
                                                                                                                                                         
  The honest answer is: the PLATFORM is potentially a startup. The ZK verifier is the moat.                                                              
   
  Here's the real value proposition: imagine you're a research lab. You want to pay $10,000 USDC to whoever can solve this protein folding sub-problem.  
  With existing platforms (Kaggle, Gitcoin, etc.) you have to trust the platform operator to judge submissions honestly. With this, you write a checker
  in Rust, lock the vkeyHash on-chain, put $10,000 in escrow, and walk away. The math pays the winner automatically. No judges. No disputes. No platform 
  operator involvement.                                               

  That's actually new. That's the thing that doesn't exist.                                                                                              
   
  What makes it hard as a startup:                                                                                                                       
  - Proof generation UX is rough (install toolchain, 16GB RAM, 2 min per proof)
  - Most "bounty" use cases don't need ZK — they need human judgment ("is this essay good?")                                                             
  - SP1 isn't audited for production yet (their docs say so explicitly)                     
  - You'd be competing with Gitcoin, Bountysource, etc. who have network effects                                                                         
                                                                                                                                                         
  What makes it potentially work:                                                                                                                        
  - AI agents don't care about UX friction — they'll just run the prover                                                                                 
  - The TAM is "any computational problem where you want trustless verification" — huge                                                                  
  - As agent-to-agent economies grow, you need machine-verifiable bounties, not human judges                                                             
  - First mover in "ZK-verified agent bounties on Solana"                                                                                                
                                                                                                                                                         
  The honest pitch: this is a developer tool for the agent economy, not a consumer product. The right market is AI research labs, prediction markets, and
   complex technical bounties where human judging is either too slow, too expensive, or too corruptible.                                                 
                                                                                                                                                         
  Is it the holy grail? For a specific niche — yes. Is it a billion dollar startup on its own? Probably not yet. Is it a genuinely cool technical        
  primitive that could be worth a lot as part of a larger agent economy platform? Absolutely.
